Watching a tiny model learn to check its own work

The same four-number arithmetic puzzle, answered by three successive versions of a 0.5-billion-parameter model. Every generation below is verbatim — nothing edited, only trimmed with “…”. Read left to right to see reasoning appear, then sharpen.

raw Qwen2.5-0.5B→ SFT cold-start→ + GRPO

What GRPO added

Per-sample solve rate on 100 held-out instances (800 attempts). GRPO lifted reliability ~10 points over a strong supervised baseline — a gain only a large eval could resolve.

The gain is policy sharpening: GRPO became more reliable per attempt (60.4% vs 50.9%) but solved fewer distinct instances at least once (70 vs 82 of 100). Reward optimization concentrates probability on dependable solutions and spends some exploration breadth — entropy reduction, visible in the numbers.

What's honestly true

  • GRPO measurably helped — +~10 points per-sample solve over a strong SFT baseline, on 800 held-out attempts. The gain was invisible on a small eval and only appeared with enough trials.
  • This is R1-style cold-start + RL, not reasoning from nothing. The SFT stage seeded the form of step-by-step reasoning (including the try-check-retry demonstrations); GRPO refined it and the solving ability.
  • 0.5B has a ceiling. The hardest instances are never solved, and degenerate loops still occur. ~60% on four-number Countdown is a credible small-model result, not a frontier one.
  • Every piece — the LoRA adapters, the verifiable reward, the group-relative advantage, the KL penalty — was hand-written in PyTorch. No RL libraries.
